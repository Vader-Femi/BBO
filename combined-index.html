Q3: What are the duties of the bank to their customer?
A: The duties of the banker include:
  • The bank undertakes to receive money and settle bills for its customer's account [cite: 5].
  • The bank borrows the proceeds and undertakes to repay them [cite: 5].
  • The bank will not cease to do business with the customer except upon reasonable notice [cite: 5].
  • The bank is not liable to pay until the customer demands payment from the bank [cite: 5].
  • The bank should only make such payments as the customer has authorized (Pay according to Customers Instruction) [cite: 5].
  • The bank should not refuse to make payments up to the limit of the customer's credit balance [cite: 5].
  • The banker has a duty to keep in confidence what he/she knows about the customer's affairs (Confidentiality) [cite: 5].
  • The bank has a duty to provide the customer on request with a statement of account [cite: 5].

Q12: Differentiate between current and savings account.
A: The key operational differences include:
  • A Current Account is subject to a Current Account Maintenance Fee (CAMF) applied to customer induced debit transactions [cite: 5].
  • A Savings account is not subject to CAMF for withdrawals or transfers [cite: 5].
  • A Savings account cannot be thrown into debit [cite: 5].
  • Current accounts can accommodate loan repayments without incurring the CAMF charge [cite: 5].

Q22: Why is it important for a teller to resume earlier than other staff?
A: Tellers should resume earlier than 7:30am to:
  • Switch on their computer and get ready for work [cite: 5].
  • Review their compartment and ensure no cash was left in the cubicle [cite: 5].
  • Pick their till boxes from the vault [cite: 5].

Q23: What is the sequence of activities tellers must perform at the end of the day?
A: The end-of-day sequence includes:
  • The Teller balances their position by checking the balance on the system and comparing it to the balance of cash on hand [cite: 5].
  • If there is a difference, they print out the transaction journal and do a call over to resolve the error, escalating to a supervisor if it persists [cite: 5].
  • Complete the proof sheet to show the summary of activities for the day [cite: 5].
  • Make up a batch by enveloping all the instruments [cite: 5].
  • Complete a teller transfer slip to transfer (sell) any cash in the cubicle back to the vault [cite: 5].
  • Evacuate cash into the vault with the Vault Custodian [cite: 5].
  • Return loose cash, stamps, and proofs into the till box, lock it, and sign the vault record book alongside the Vault Custodian [cite: 5].
Q24: Who are the key personnel involved in vault operations and what is the importance of dual control?

  • The Vault must be under Dual Control to ensure that all cash movements from any of the stakeholders are done securely and in line with the Bank policy [cite: 5].
Q25: Differentiate between a cash shortage and a cash overage.
A:
  • Cash Shortage: A situation whereby the physical cash with the Teller is less than the balance shown by the system and Teller Proof (often due to overpayment to customers or pilfering) [cite: 5].
  • Cash Overage (Surplus): A situation whereby the physical cash with the Teller is more than the balance shown by the system and Teller Proof (often due to deliberate or genuine under-payment to customers) [cite: 5].
Q26: How do you process a deposit tagged 'Subject to Count' (STC)?
A: To process an STC deposit:
  • Ensure a legally signed and executed Subject to Count agreement is in place between the bank and the customer [cite: 5].
  • Review the Deposit Slip for regularity [cite: 5].
  • Bundle count the cash deposits [cite: 5].
  • Pass entries immediately: Debit the Cash Account and Credit the customer's account immediately [cite: 5].
  • Release the acknowledgement slip to the customer [cite: 5].
  • Count the cash in detail within 24 hours [cite: 5].
  • Debit/Credit the customer's account under advice for any discovered shortages/overages [cite: 5].
Q27: How do you handle a late cash deposit?
A: Handling a late deposit requires the teller to:
  • Review the Deposit Slip for regularity [cite: 5].
  • Verify (detail count) cash in the presence of the depositor [cite: 5].
  • Machine stamp and write or rubber stamp "late deposit for credit on (date)" on the deposit slip [cite: 5].
  • Advise the depositor that credit to their account will be made the following business day [cite: 5].
  • Obtain the Cash Officer's/Supervisor's initials on the deposit slip and the Late Cash register [cite: 5].
  • Release the acknowledgement copy to the depositor [cite: 5].
  • Record Cash in the Teller Proof as Late Cash and place the deposit in the cash box/vault [cite: 5].
  • Process the deposit the following business day [cite: 5].

Q31: What is a dud check?
A:
  • A dud check is one issued to induce the delivery of any asset or property, or to settle a lawful obligation, which when presented within a reasonable period of time is dishonoured (bounced) on grounds of lack of funds or insufficient funds in the account of the Issuer [cite: 5].

Q35: What is SWIFT and what is RTGS?
A:
  • SWIFT: The Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication. It is a highly secure platform used for processing foreign currency transactions and settlements between correspondent banks [cite: 5].
  • RTGS: Real Time Gross Settlement. It is a settlement system where the transfer of large funds or securities between banks is executed on a "real time" (without delay) and "gross" (one-to-one) basis [cite: 5].
Q36: What is the bank's risk exposure on a clean Commercial Paper, and why do investors prefer guaranteed ones?
A:
  • Commercial Papers (CP) are completely unsecured unless a Bank adds its Guarantee, thereby becoming a Guaranteed Commercial Paper (GCP) [cite: 5].
  • On a clean CP, the customer is the primary obligor, leaving the investor exposed to default risk [cite: 5].
  • Investors prefer guaranteed ones because the bank becomes a secondary obligor, providing a formal guarantee of payment [cite: 5].
Q37: Explain Open Buy Back (OBB) and Fixed Buy Back (FBB).
A:
  • Both are inter-bank placements or takings that are secured by Treasury Bills [cite: 5].
  • Open Buy Back (OBB): Has a flexible timing (as short as 1 day/overnight or as long as 91 days). Being "open", either the counterparty or the bank can call for its deposit back at any time [cite: 5].
  • Fixed Buy Back (FBB): Funds are pledged to the counterparty for a strictly fixed period, meaning the counterparty cannot call for its deposit back at any time [cite: 5].

Q41: Explain the following controls: Segregation of Duties, Authorization, Authorization Limits, Written Mandates, and Confirmation.
A:
  • Segregation of Duties: One person must not be allowed to initiate a transaction and conclude it; initiation, confirmation, recording, and settlement must be separated [cite: 5].
  • Authorization: The staff that prepares the source document should not be the one to approve or authorize its posting [cite: 5].
  • Authorization Limits: Setting limits in figures for staff, dictating what amounts signatories can approve and what junior/senior dealers can handle [cite: 5].
  • Written Mandates: All instructions must be in writing; staff should not implement verbal instructions even from top Management as they can be denied [cite: 5].
  • Confirmation: Due to their magnitude, all TROPS transactions must be independently confirmed before processing [cite: 5].

Case Study 1: How do you process a Zenith Bank check for 5 million Naira paid into an Access Bank account?
A:
  • This is processed as an Other Banks' Cheques (OBC) transaction because it is drawn on another bank but payable to our customer [cite: 5].
  • It will be processed through the Clearing House with a forward value date (T+1) [cite: 5].
Case Study 2: How do you treat an Access Bank check for 3 million Naira paid into another Access Bank account?
A:
  • It is processed internally as an In-House Account-To-Account Transfer since both the drawer and payee are domiciled with the same Drawee bank [cite: 5].
Case Study 3: A customer pays a UBA check for 10.1 million Naira. How is it processed?
A:
  • Because the amount exceeds the maximum limit for cheques (N10 million fixed by the CBN), it cannot be cleared via standard cheque truncation [cite: 5].
  • The customer must utilize electronic channels like RTGS to transfer the funds [cite: 5].
Case Study 4: What platform should be used to debit an account for 7.5 million Naira to pay salaries for 550 staff?
A:
  • Bulk NEFT Credits should be used, as they are Automated Direct Credits designed specifically to facilitate paperless clearing of bulk credits like salaries [cite: 5].
Case Study 5: What platform is used to debit an account for 75 million Naira and purchase $75,000 for transfer?
A:
  • SWIFT is used for processing the foreign currency transactions and settlement instructions offshore [cite: 5].
  • RTGS is used for the high-value Naira settlement [cite: 5].
Case Study 6: Assume Access Bank wants to buy $150,000 from First Bank. What platform will Access Bank use?
A:
  • Access Bank will use RTGS to execute the real-time interbank Naira settlement [cite: 5].
  • Access Bank will use SWIFT to process the foreign exchange settlement [cite: 5].
Case Study 7: What platform should a customer use to transfer 135,000 Naira to a secondary school for fees?
A:
  • The customer should use Single Item NEFT (which covers individual transfers like tuition payments) or NIBSS Instant Payment (NIP) [cite: 5].
